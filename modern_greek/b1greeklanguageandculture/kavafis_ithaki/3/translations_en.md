### Ιθάκη, στ. 13–23

Wish that the road be long.
May the summer mornings be many
when with what pleasure, with what joy
you will enter harbors never seen before;
to stop at Phoenician trading posts,
and to acquire the fine goods,
mother-of-pearl and corals, amber and ebony,
and sensuous perfumes of every kind,
sensuous perfumes as abundantly as you can;
to go to many Egyptian cities,
to learn and to learn from the learned ones.

## Valassopoulo

<!-- **G. Valassopoulo, 1924** · the first published English translation, in *The Criterion* (July 1924) · public domain in the US · text via [ornaverum.org](https://ornaverum.org/misc/epilogue/06.html) -->

### Ιθάκη, στ. 13–23

Wish that the way be long,
that on many summer mornings,
with great pleasure, great delight,
you enter harbours for the first time seen;
that you stop at Phoenician marts,
and procure the goodly merchandise,
mother-of-pearl and corals, amber and ebony,
and sensual perfumes of all kinds,
plenty of sensual perfumes especially;
to wend your way to many Egyptian cities,
to learn and yet to learn from the wise.
