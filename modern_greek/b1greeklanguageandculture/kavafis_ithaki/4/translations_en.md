## literal

<!-- **literal** · a literal English rendering written for this course from the Greek text and its word-by-word gloss, line for line with the Greek -->

### Ιθάκη, στ. 24–30

Always have Ithaca in your mind.
Arriving there is your destination.
But do not hurry the journey at all.
Better that it last many years;
and as an old man by then to moor at the island,
rich with all that you gained on the road,
not expecting Ithaca to give you riches.

### Ιθάκη, στ. 31–33

Ithaca gave you the beautiful journey.
Without her you would not have set out on the road.
Other things she does not have to give you any more.

### Ιθάκη, στ. 34–36

And if you find her poor, Ithaca did not deceive you.
So wise as you have become, with such experience,
already you will have understood what the Ithacas mean.

## Valassopoulo

<!-- **G. Valassopoulo, 1924** · the first published English translation, in *The Criterion* (July 1924) · public domain in the US · text via [ornaverum.org](https://ornaverum.org/misc/epilogue/06.html) -->

### Ιθάκη, στ. 24–30

Ever keep Ithaca in your mind,
your return thither is your goal.
But do not hasten at all your voyage,
better that it last for many years;
And full of years at length you anchor at your isle
rich with all that you gained on the way;
do not expect Ithaca to give you riches.

### Ιθάκη, στ. 31–33

Ithaca gave you your fair voyage.
Without her you would not have ventured on the way.
But she has no more to give you.

### Ιθάκη, στ. 34–36

And if you find Ithaca a poor place, she has not mocked you.
You have become so wise, so full of experience
that you should understand already what these Ithacas mean.
